# Why most prompting advice is noise

Search for prompt engineering and you will find threads promising secret phrases, packs of "500 god tier prompts" for sale, and confident people telling you to threaten the model or offer it a tip. Almost none of it holds up.

I want to save you that detour. This lesson clears the noise so the next four can give you the small handful of things that genuinely move results.

## The stuff that does not work

Why do these keep spreading? Because they are memorable and easy to sell, and because people credit the magic phrase when the real improvement came from something boring in the same prompt, usually being more specific.

## The stuff that does work

Here is the honest list. It is short, and none of it is clever:

1. **Say exactly what you want.** Specific beats vague, every time.
2. **Explain why you want it.** Reasons travel further than rules.
3. **Show an example.** One good example beats a paragraph of description.
4. **Steer instead of starting over.** The second ask is where the quality is.

That is the whole course. Four lessons, one idea each.

## The mental model that makes it click

Anthropic describe Claude as a brilliant but new employee who lacks context on your norms and workflows. I have not found a better way to put it.

Think about what that means. On a new colleague's first day you would never say "make me a report" and expect the right thing. They do not know your audience, your format, your standards, or what happens to the work next. They are not lacking intelligence. They are lacking context. So you tell them.

That is the whole job. Not magic words. Context.

## The golden rule

Anthropic offer a test I now use constantly, and it is the single most useful idea in this whole course:

> Show your prompt to a colleague with minimal context on the task and ask them to follow it. If they would be confused, Claude will be too.

That is it. If a reasonable human with no background could not act on your request without asking a follow up question, your prompt is not finished. Every technique in the next four lessons is really just a way of passing this test.

<Callout type="tip" title="Try this now">
  Take the last thing you asked an AI that disappointed you. Read it back as if you were a new colleague who has never met you. What would you need to ask before you could start? That answer is exactly what your prompt was missing.
</Callout>
